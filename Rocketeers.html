    Why we built PlateShare

    Every day, good food is thrown away while people nearby go without a meal. We wanted to close that gap.

    The problem

    Around the world, roughly a billion tonnes of food are wasted every year. Much of it is still fresh and safe to eat: the extra biryani after a lunch rush, the trays left over from a wedding, the bread a bakery cannot sell by closing time. Kitchens usually want to give it away, but they have no quick way to reach someone who can collect it before it spoils. So it goes in the bin.

    Why we made this

    We started PlateShare as a hackathon project because the fix felt simple. Donors already know what they have left and when it must be collected. NGOs, hostels and neighbours already know who needs a meal. What was missing was a fast, friendly place for the two to meet. We wanted posting food to take seconds and claiming it to take one tap, so that helping never feels like extra work for a busy kitchen.

    Our goal

    To turn surplus food into meals, not waste, and to make sharing it an everyday habit in every neighbourhood. This supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 12, Responsible Consumption and Production.

    How it works

    1. Share: a donor posts what is left, how many meals, where, and a pickup deadline.
    2. Claim: an NGO or neighbour taps Claim and gets a 4-digit pickup code.
    3. Rescue: they collect it before the deadline, and the meal counts toward our impact.

    Where we are going

    This is a working prototype today. Next we want to add verified donors, food-safety guidelines, accounts and a real database, and then bring more restaurants, hostels and caterers on board. Want to help? Get in touch.

    Hygiene matters

    Sharing food is only a good deed if the food is safe. These short rules keep every meal fit to eat.

    For donors

    • Share only food that is fresh, properly cooked and has not been served or touched by others.
    • Wash your hands and use clean gloves, spoons and containers.
    • Pack in clean, covered, food-grade containers, and label what it is and when it was cooked.
    • Keep hot food hot and cold food cold until pickup, and set a realistic pickup deadline.
    • List common allergens such as nuts, dairy, gluten and eggs, and mark veg or non-veg correctly.

    For receivers

    • Collect on time and bring clean, covered containers or bags.
    • Keep the food covered and away from raw items while you carry it.
    • Serve it soon after pickup. Do not keep it for later.
    • If the food smells, looks or feels off, do not serve it, and let the donor know.

    Quick safety numbers

    2 hourslongest cooked food should sit at room temperature
    60°C+keep hot food at or above this
    5°C or lesskeep cold food at or below this
    20 secwash hands with soap before handling food

    In hot weather, aim for an hour or less at room temperature. These are general guidelines. Please also follow your local food-safety rules, such as FSSAI in India.

    When in doubt, leave it out

    If you are not sure a dish is safe, do not post it. PlateShare is a prototype and does not yet check donors or inspect food, so donors and receivers share the responsibility for safety.

    Quick answers

    How do I share food?

    Open Share food, fill in the item, number of meals, pickup deadline and area, then post. It appears on Find food straight away.

    How does claiming work?

    Tap Claim on a listing. You get a 4-digit pickup code to show the donor, and the listing is marked as taken so nobody else turns up for it.

    Is it free?

    Yes, it is free for donors and receivers.

    How is food safety handled?

    Donors set a pickup deadline on every listing, and we ask everyone to follow the simple rules on the Hygiene page. Donor verification is planned for the full version.

    Is this a finished product?

    It is a working prototype built for a hackathon. Listings are stored in your browser, and a real launch would add accounts and a database.
